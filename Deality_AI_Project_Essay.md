# Deality AI: Making Online Product Comparisons Clearer

Shopping online gives people many choices, but useful information is scattered across retailers and often incomplete. One listing may show a price and rating while another includes only a product name and a few features. A lower price alone does not necessarily mean a product is the best fit, and a comparison can be misleading when currencies differ or important details are missing. Deality AI was developed as a Streamlit prototype to help shoppers organize the evidence they can see, compare several offers, and understand when that evidence is not enough to name a winner.

## Building a retailer-independent comparison tool

The project uses SerpApi’s Google Shopping Results API with the `google_shopping` engine to find offers. Shoppers search separately for each option, then choose from the returned results. The application is designed to compare between two and five products and can also accept details entered manually when a suitable search result is unavailable. A search phrase can reuse a result cached for the current Streamlit session, avoiding an unnecessary repeat search during that session.

The app presents available listing information in a side-by-side comparison: product name, retailer, price, rating, review count, and any returned features or specifications. It can show retailer or Shopping links when those are present and pass conservative checks. It does not open pasted retailer URLs, follow redirects to discover a merchant page, or claim to have verified a product-detail page. Missing fields are shown as unavailable rather than filled in with guesses.

Because two similar-looking listings may not actually serve the same purpose, the application uses heuristic product-category checks. A clearly detected mismatch—such as comparing a watch with a computer—is blocked. If the category is uncertain, the shopper must confirm that the selected products serve the same purpose. These checks are safeguards, not proof of comparability; shoppers remain responsible for selecting genuinely comparable products.

## Recommendations that explain their limits

A central design decision was to make the answer available immediately after the shopper presses Compare. Deality AI now calculates a deterministic recommendation using the selected priority and comparison statistics. Options include price-focused and rating-focused priorities, review count, and several specification-oriented views. Price scoring requires valid prices with known matching currency codes. The app does not convert currencies, treat review count as product quality, or assign invented numeric scores to product specifications and features.

The recommendation can name a leading option when the selected data supports a comparison. It can also state that no winner is established—for example, when the relevant information is missing, scores are tied, currencies cannot be compared, or the chosen priority depends on unscored specification evidence. This is intentional: a useful shopping assistant should provide a grounded answer, but it should not manufacture certainty just to always choose a product.

The Deality AI chat is available inline with the comparison instead of behind a popover or a provider-selection menu. When `OPENAI_API_KEY` is configured, open-ended questions use OpenAI with bounded listing details and recent conversation as context. Direct price and weight questions retain deterministic answers, and rule-based answers remain available when no key is configured or OpenAI is unavailable. Product URLs and image URLs are not sent to the model. This replaced earlier Gemini and Ollama paths after provider availability and hosting limitations made those implementations unreliable. Gemini-specific code, configuration, tests, and setup instructions were removed. The chat is placed beside the immediate result so shoppers can find and use it without hunting through the page.

## A separate experiment in preference modeling

The repository also contains earlier Deality Match experiments using scikit-learn, TF-IDF text features, and logistic regression. The training scripts combine an original laptop pilot with additional laptop scenarios and user-supplied scenarios from other categories. The combined experimental dataset contains 71 candidate listings across 29 scenarios. Evaluation holds out whole scenarios rather than splitting candidates from one scenario across training and testing. Across these scenarios, leave-one-scenario-out candidate-label accuracy was 0.521 and the selected top choice matched the provided preference in 15 of 29 scenarios. Source-specific top-choice results were 0/3 for the original pilot, 6/11 for additional laptop scenarios, and 12/15 for the small set of other-category scenarios.

Those numbers are not evidence of a reliable general-purpose shopping model. The examples are small, user-provided, and unverified, and non-laptop categories have very few scenarios. The classifier is retained as an offline experiment and is not shown in or called by the current app. The interface was simplified so shoppers are not presented with a laptop-heavy experimental model as if it were a broadly validated product recommender.

A different offline experiment uses 20 synthetic user scenarios and 60 candidate rows across laptops, watches, hoodies, and musical instruments. It reserves four entire scenarios for validation and keeps those scenarios out of training. Its candidate-label accuracy was 1.000 on training data and 0.667 on validation data; its scenario top-choice results were 16/16 on training scenarios and 1/4 on validation scenarios. These are synthetic-example results, not evidence of real-world recommendation quality, and this separate artifact is also not used by the app.

## Interface, privacy, and deployment

The interface uses Deality AI branding and a blue, purple, cyan, and pink gradient design. Recent refinements removed redundant warning blocks and moved the comparison result toward the top of the page, while retaining concise explanations that search listings are unverified and missing evidence may prevent a supported winner. The visible assistant no longer requires users to choose an AI provider or install a model.

Live search phrases are sent to SerpApi and are subject to that service’s policies and quota. When configured, OpenAI also receives the shopper’s question, bounded conversation, and selected listing names, prices, ratings, reviews, descriptions, features, and specifications. Product and image URLs are excluded. OpenAI API usage may incur charges and follows that provider’s data policies. Product selections, cached results, comparison state, and chat are held in the active Streamlit session rather than written to a Deality database. A clear control removes the current searches, comparison, and chat. API credentials belong in local `.env` or the hosting platform’s secrets store; they should never be committed to public source control or shared in screenshots.

The project was published to the public GitHub repository `saikhushal2012-gif/Deality-Ai`, and an initial Streamlit Community Cloud deployment loaded successfully. Local follow-up changes need to be reviewed, committed, and pushed to the main branch before the hosted app can redeploy with them. Live search requires a SerpApi key. Hosted chat requires `OPENAI_API_KEY` in local `.env` or Streamlit Community Cloud Secrets; without it, deterministic answers remain available.

## Testing and lessons learned

The project includes unit tests for shopping-result normalization, comparison rules, currency and missing-data behavior, category compatibility, manual listings, chat answers, and Streamlit app flows. Network-dependent search and model interactions are mocked in tests so that the test suite does not rely on live offers or consume API quota. Compilation and repeated test runs have been used during development; the final full-suite result should be confirmed after the latest interface changes are committed.

Several lessons shaped the finished direction. First, missing information is itself important and should remain visible. Second, price comparisons need currency context. Third, a review count is not a quality score. Fourth, recommendation interfaces should put the answer where the user expects it and avoid presenting experimental metrics as proof of suitability. Finally, a feature that depends on a hosted service or a model installed only on a user’s computer is not dependable on a separate cloud host. A transparent rules-based fallback is more useful than a broken button, provided it clearly reports when the available evidence cannot support a winner.

## Conclusion

Deality AI brings multi-retailer search, manual listing entry, compatibility checks, side-by-side details, deterministic recommendations, and a visible evidence-based assistant into one application. Its goal is not to guarantee that a product is right for every shopper, but to make available information easier to inspect and to distinguish a supported comparison from an uncertain one. The experiments in preference classification remain documented as experiments rather than being presented as a validated production model. As a prototype, Deality AI still needs broader verified data, more real-world evaluation, and ongoing checks of current retailer offers and deployment behavior. It is not financial advice, and shoppers should verify price, availability, shipping, returns, and product details with the retailer before purchasing.
